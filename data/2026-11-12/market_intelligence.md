It’s been quite clear recently that once yields hit the 5%level, they started attracting capital back. But what’s interesting? Everyone says capital is flowing back into the bond market, but I’ve noticed that it’s not just flowing in indiscriminately; it’s being selective. For instance, within government bonds, short-term bonds are outperforming long-term ones. Even if you're talking about a stagflationary recession, capital wouldn't be moving into the bond market anyway, because it would shift toward holding cash. The current situation is more like what we’ve discussed many times: the market has entered a period of scrutiny, and it’s focusing on better credit quality. So, if I have some doubts, while I wait for more convincing results or data to emerge, I want to park my capital in assets that offer a certain level of yield. I think that's the current approach.
